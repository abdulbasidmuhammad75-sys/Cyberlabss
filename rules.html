RESPONSIBLE CYBERSECURITY

CyberLab Safety & Ethics

Learn cybersecurity responsibly. Use your knowledge to protect systems, understand networks, improve privacy, and defend people from digital threats.

⚠️ AUTHORIZED PRACTICAL USE ONLY: CyberLab practical activities must be performed only on your own devices, your own accounts, or systems where you have clear permission. Never use cybersecurity tools to invade another person's privacy.

Our Six Core Rules

01

Get Authorization

Always receive clear permission before testing a device, website, account, network, or application.

  • Test your own systems.
  • Use approved learning laboratories.
  • Respect the limits of permission.
02

Respect Privacy

Personal information belongs to people. Do not collect, expose, share, or monitor private data without permission.

  • Protect passwords and accounts.
  • Do not access private messages.
  • Keep sensitive information confidential.
03

Understand Location Data

IP geolocation is approximate. Exact device GPS requires the device owner's permission through a supported location service.

  • IP location is not exact GPS.
  • Never track people secretly.
  • Explain location limitations honestly.
04

Use Safe Labs

Practice inside controlled environments designed for learning. Avoid testing systems that could be damaged or interrupted.

  • Use legal training platforms.
  • Keep experiments isolated.
  • Back up important files first.
05

Responsible Disclosure

If you discover a security weakness, report it privately to the responsible owner instead of publicly exposing it.

  • Document the issue carefully.
  • Contact the correct organization.
  • Give the owner time to respond.
06

Think Like a Defender

The purpose of cybersecurity knowledge is to reduce harm and strengthen protection, not to damage systems or people.

  • Protect before attacking.
  • Learn how threats work.
  • Use skills for positive results.

Practice or Stop?

✅ Safe CyberLab Practice

These activities are suitable when you have permission:

  • Checking your own public IP information.
  • Viewing your own device GPS after permission.
  • Learning how DNS and networks work.
  • Testing a website you own.
  • Studying security concepts in a training lab.
  • Improving your own passwords and privacy settings.

🛑 Stop and Check Permission

Do not continue when an activity involves:

  • Accessing another person's account.
  • Tracking someone secretly.
  • Stealing passwords or private data.
  • Disrupting a website or network.
  • Installing hidden monitoring software.
  • Testing a system without authorization.

Core Principle

A good cybersecurity learner does not ask only, “Can I do this?” A responsible learner also asks, “Do I have permission, and could this harm someone?”
